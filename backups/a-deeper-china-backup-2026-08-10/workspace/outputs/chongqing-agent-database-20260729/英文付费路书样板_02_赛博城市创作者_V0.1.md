# Cyberpunk With Consequences

## A two-day Chongqing roadbook for an independent creator

**Pilot sample — not a filming permit or an operational production plan.** Commercial use, equipment, public-space rules and location permissions must be confirmed before release.

### Your stated goal

You have two days and want cyberpunk images for YouTube.

### What your request implies—but does not prove

It implies that light, timing, movement and visual contrast matter more than conventional sightseeing. It does **not** prove that:

- your filming is non-commercial;
- a tripod is allowed everywhere;
- a guide can obtain permits;
- residents have consented to appear;
- drone footage seen online was legally produced.

Before route design, I would ask for:

1. team size and equipment;
2. commercial or personal use;
3. tripod, lighting and audio requirements;
4. drone plans;
5. whether faces or private businesses must appear;
6. tolerance for crowds, stairs and late-night return.

## Day 1 — The system behind the image

### Narrative

“Cyberpunk Chongqing” is not a single style applied to a skyline. The image comes from multiple ground levels, infrastructure inserted into dense terrain, bridges spanning large vertical gaps, and old circulation systems meeting newer urban renewal.

### Shot sequence

**1. Kuixinglou: establish multiple ground levels**

The shot is not “person on a bridge”. The sequence should reveal uncertainty:

1. begin on what feels like an ordinary street;
2. reveal the height relationship;
3. show how another circulation path meets the same level;
4. finish with a human-scale wayfinding detail.

**Production boundary:** no promise of private rooftops, restricted edges or exclusive access.

**2. Chaotianmen: rebuild the two-river geometry**

Use the river junction to establish the city’s physical logic before photographing the skyline. If the sky is flat or visibility poor, shift from wide skyline shots to:

- retaining walls;
- bridge layers;
- vertical circulation;
- river-level versus ridge-level movement.

**3. Midday production break**

Back up footage, cool equipment, recharge batteries and reassess weather. In high summer, this is also the primary heat-risk control.

**4. Hongyadong and the opposite river side**

Use Hongyadong as one illuminated layer within a larger section of bridge, cliff, road and tower. Do not promise an uninterrupted walk through a holiday control zone.

### Blue-hour decision

Choose only one:

- a fixed public river viewpoint;
- a bridge/river relationship shot;
- a closer vertical-circulation sequence.

Do not schedule a cruise, cableway and second panorama after the same sunset. They compete for the same light and reduce production reliability.

### Drone boundary

Official event notices identify the Grand Theatre and Chaotianmen area as part of airport airspace protection and prohibit unauthorized drone flights. This roadbook does not provide a “safe place to take off”. It provides the official query and approval questions that must be resolved.

## Day 2 — Transit as an engineering response

### Narrative

The aim is to replace the cliché “a train goes through a building” with a more useful observation: the monorail follows curves, slopes and a constrained urban corridor, while stations connect to different street levels.

### Shot sequence

**1. Ride Line 2 before going to the viewing platform**

Capture:

- the shift from underground/contained space to elevated track;
- river-edge alignment;
- curves and close urban interfaces;
- passengers using the system as ordinary transport.

Do not block doors, photograph individuals closely without consent or treat the carriage as a controlled set.

**2. Liziba: construction-aware decision**

The official notice states that work between June and November 2026 affects platform areas, halls and several exits in phases, with overall works expected to continue into December.

Therefore:

- the train ride remains the core;
- the external platform is conditional;
- no exit number is published without a same-day check;
- the crew follows staff instructions and current signs.

**3. Eling: only if visibility justifies a high point**

If fog obscures the skyline, use the high point to reconstruct the route rather than forcing a failed panorama.

**4. Cave or industrial layer**

Add one verified interior/industrial-space module only if opening and filming rules are confirmed. The purpose is to introduce material texture and another time layer, not to add another “viral” location.

## Production risk card

| Risk | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Commercial use | Confirm whether location permission or a contract is required |
| Tripod/light stand | Confirm obstruction and venue rules |
| Residents and shop staff | Obtain individual and venue permission where needed |
| Drone | Check official airspace and approval requirements; do not infer legality from social media |
| Holiday crowd control | Use a fixed public position and a preselected exit |
| Extreme heat | Move exterior production to early/late windows |
| Fog or rain | Switch from skyline to infrastructure details |
| Construction | Preserve the train sequence and delete unverified exits |

## What the paid roadbook adds

- a shot sequence tied to one city argument;
- live construction and crowd checks;
- a route-level equipment and heat budget;
- explicit consent and filming boundaries;
- a bad-weather substitution that preserves the story;
- a “do not shoot / do not promise” list.

It does not sell access the operator does not control.
