# A Deeper China | Hangzhou draft

**Shape:** 2 days, built around West Lake as a slow landscape · Shanghai–Hangzhou pacing decision · Tea as landscape, labour and taste.

## Why this Hangzhou
This is not Shanghai with a lake attached. It uses water, hills, tea and sacred landscape to give the traveller a real change of pace.

## Suggested building blocks
- **West Lake as a slow landscape** — How do I experience West Lake without treating it as a lap around a postcard?
- **Shanghai–Hangzhou pacing decision** — Is Hangzhou a day trip, an overnight, or a destination in its own right?
- **Tea as landscape, labour and taste** — What does Longjing mean beyond buying tea or staging a ceremony?

## On-the-ground notes
- **West Lake intention card:** West Lake has different meanings for a walker, photographer, garden reader, first visitor and someone wanting quiet; a generic circuit serves none well. **Check:** weather, crowds, walking_distance.
- **West Lake morning/evening decision:** Time of day changes crowd, heat and light more than adding a second landmark. **Check:** weather, crowds, transport.
- **Shanghai day-trip decision:** A day trip can work for one carefully bounded lake/city layer, but not West Lake, tea, Lingyin and a relaxed return all at once. **Check:** rail, transfer, weather, overpacking.
- **Hangzhou overnight decision:** An overnight makes room for low-crowd timing and reduces the need to convert landscape into a transit challenge. **Check:** hotel_area, weather, arrival_time.
- **Longjing tea landscape decision:** Tea fields can connect landscape, seasonal agriculture and taste, but they are not a guaranteed harvest, ceremony or shopping experience. **Check:** seasonality, weather, transport, commercialization.
- **Tea purchase boundary:** A roadbook can help a visitor ask better questions about tea, but cannot authenticate quality, price, provenance or seller claims. **Check:** quality, price, provenance, language.
