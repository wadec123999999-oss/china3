# A Deeper China | Chengdu draft

**Shape:** 3 days, built around The Chengdu rhythm · Pandas as a real operational decision · Tea, park and public life · Sichuan flavour as social geography.

## Why this Chengdu
This is not a panda-and-hotpot checklist. It gives one strong anchor, a food and public-life rhythm, and enough unplanned time for Chengdu to work as a city.

## Suggested building blocks
- **The Chengdu rhythm** — How do I experience Chengdu without turning it into a rush between attractions?
- **Pandas as a real operational decision** — Is the Panda Base worth my time and how should it shape the day?
- **Tea, park and public life** — Where does Chengdu's slow-city feeling become legible?
- **Sichuan flavour as social geography** — How do I eat Sichuan food without being sent to a generic tourist list?

## On-the-ground notes
- **Kuanzhai Alleys decision:** A legible historic-commercial district that may suit a first-time visitor but can conflict with a client seeking less retail noise. **Check:** crowds, retail_noise, photo_expectations.
- **Arrival and Metro setup:** Independent travel begins with payment, data, mapping and a realistic first-day energy estimate. **Check:** payment, connectivity, app_setup, jetlag.
- **Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding:** For many first-time international visitors it is the strongest single reason to include Chengdu, but it should consume only the part of a day it genuinely needs. **Check:** reservation, passport_match, crowds, weather, animal_activity.
- **Panda Base versus Dujiangyan Panda Valley decision:** The choice changes the entire day: city convenience versus a wider day-trip purpose. **Check:** transport, reservation, weather, day_length.
- **People's Park tea-time structure:** A public-space lens for Chengdu's rhythm: sitting, watching and allowing the city to unfold rather than moving through a sight list. **Check:** weather, crowds, venue_turnover.
- **Hotpot / chuanchuan meal logic:** The value is shared decision-making and a local meal format, not a generic 'must-eat' restaurant ranking. **Check:** spice, dietary_needs, queues, ordering_language.
- **Sichuan snacks and tea as a low-commitment food layer:** Lets a visitor approach flavour with smaller commitments when a communal hotpot meal is not a fit. **Check:** ingredients, allergens, translation.
