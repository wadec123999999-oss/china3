# 上海海外首发内容脚本包 01｜Two Days in Shanghai: What to Leave Out

## 内容目标

用一个反直觉命题吸引“第一次来中国/上海、只有两三天”的英语独立旅行者：

> `The best two-day Shanghai plan is not the one that fits the most in.`

它证明 A Deeper China 的价值是判断与取舍，不是“我们比别人知道更多景点”。

## 一条内容的边界

- 讲城市尺度、到达疲劳、主线与备选线；
- 不给实时开放、票务、餐厅、渡轮或交通保证；
- 不把老城、宗教场所、居民区当猎奇画面；
- 不拍可识别的儿童、私宅内景、宗教仪式；
- 所有出现的动态地点信息以实际拍摄当天的现场规则为准。

## B. Short 02｜雨天不是失败（30 秒）

**Hook**：`A rainy day in Shanghai does not need a rescue itinerary.`

**旁白**：

`The usual mistake is replacing every outdoor plan with five indoor stops. That is just a crowded itinerary with a roof. Choose one indoor anchor near where you already are. Keep one quieter public-space alternative. Then leave time for the city to slow down with you. A good fallback is not a compromise. It is part of the route.`

**画面**：雨天路面 → 单一室内入口（不拍规则限制区域）→ 休息/窗景 → 同区域街道。

**CTA**：`Build your route with a real fallback.`

## C. Short 03｜老城不是一个景点（30 秒）

**Hook**：`Shanghai’s old city is not one more attraction to tick off.`

**旁白**：

`If you enter old Shanghai only looking for an Instagram frame, you miss the question that makes it matter: how do commerce, ritual, and daily life keep changing inside a megacity? Go slowly. Respect the places that are still lived and used. And do not force the old city, the Bund, a major museum, and a late-night cross-town plan into one day.`

**画面**：公开街巷尺度 → 生活性商业外立面 → 不拍礼拜/可识别面孔的细节 → 日落时的退出点。

**CTA**：`Choose the Shanghai you want to understand.`

## D. 7–9 分钟 YouTube 长视频

### 标题候选

1. `Shanghai in 2 Days: What to Leave Out (and Why)`
2. `The First-Time Shanghai Mistake: Planning a Checklist Instead of a City`
3. `How to Choose a Real 2-Day Shanghai Route`

### 缩略图

左右对比：左边密集地图和“12 STOPS”；右边一条简洁路线和 “ONE QUESTION / DAY”。

### 分段脚本

**0:00–0:35 — 问题**

`A two-day Shanghai itinerary often looks impressive on paper: old city, skyline, Bund, museum, French Concession, shopping street, a famous dinner, and a night view. The problem is not that any of those are wrong. The problem is that they answer different questions, in different parts of a very large city, with no room for a real arrival, a queue, a storm, or tired feet.`

**0:35–1:35 — 你不是在选择景点，而是在选择城市解释**

`For a first visit, I would begin with one of three lenses: Shanghai as an old commercial and ritual city; Shanghai as a river city shaped by trade and finance; or Shanghai as a modern cultural city of museums, streets, and changing neighborhoods. You can touch more than one. But one should lead.`

**1:35–3:15 — Lens 1：老城**

`The old city works when you want to notice scale, commerce, and the way inherited urban form survives inside a megacity. It does not work as a hurried photo stop between two reservations. Treat it as a slow observation block, and check current access and site rules before you go.`

**3:15–4:45 — Lens 2：河流城市**

`The river is not only a skyline view. It is a way to read treaty-port history, movement, finance, and the new city facing the old one. Use it when the visitor wants Shanghai’s scale. But protect energy: weather, crowds, and your return route matter more than squeezing in another crossing.`

**4:45–6:00 — Lens 3：博物馆/街区作为容错**

`Museums and walkable neighborhoods are not backup filler. They can carry the day when rain, heat, jet lag, or a reservation changes the plan. The rule is not to replace outdoor chaos with indoor chaos. Choose one anchor and keep the fallback nearby.`

**6:00–7:05 — 一个可复制框架**

`Day one: arrive gently, choose the part of the city closest to your real question, and leave early enough to recover. Day two: add a contrast, not a second checklist. And before you leave your hotel each morning, know what you will drop if the city asks more of you than expected.`

**7:05–8:10 — 透明边界与 CTA**

`This is not a promise that any site will be open, quiet, or easy. Those details change. A good independent travel plan tells you what to check, what to keep, and what to let go. If you have your dates and priorities, you can start a Shanghai trip brief with A Deeper China. You will still book everything directly. The point is to arrive with a route that thinks.`
