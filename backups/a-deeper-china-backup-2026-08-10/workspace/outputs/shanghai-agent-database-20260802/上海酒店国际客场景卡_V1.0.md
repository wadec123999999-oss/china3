# A Deeper China｜Shanghai Guest Decision Cards V1.0

**Internal pilot material for hotel / travel-designer teams.** These cards are independent-travel guidance, not operating guarantees. Staff must not promise reservations, access, tickets, transport, weather, food safety, accessibility, private experiences, or a Human-checked roadbook without the relevant current review.

## How to use a card

1. Ask the three questions first.
2. Give a route direction, not a list.
3. State the client action and dynamic check.
4. Escalate when the card says `Human review`.

---

## 01 — “We have only two days. What should we not miss?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** a city lens, not a longer list.

**Ask:**

- What are your actual arrival and departure times?
- Do you care most about old-city texture, river-city scale, or a slower cultural day?
- What would make the trip feel unsuccessful: queues, too much walking, shopping, or missing a classic view?

**Say:**

`With two real days, I would choose one main way into Shanghai and one contrast. Trying to do the old city, a major museum, the river skyline, a distant neighborhood, and a fixed dinner across town in one day usually makes the city feel like a transfer problem.`

**Do not say:** `You can easily do everything in two days.`

**Guest action:** confirm the hotel area and any venue reservation directly.

**Escalate:** Human review if dates are fixed and the guest requests a trip-specific roadbook.

## 02 — “It is raining. What should we do instead?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** a nearby, lower-friction day—not an indoor checklist.

**Ask:**

- Are you already out, or still at the hotel?
- Is this a full day, arrival day, or a day with children/older travelers?
- Would you prefer one cultural anchor or a lighter neighborhood recovery day?

**Say:**

`A good rain plan keeps one anchor close to where you already are and preserves a simpler alternative. It should reduce uncertainty, not replace outdoor rushing with five indoor stops.`

**Do not say:** `This venue will definitely be open / has no queue.`

**Guest action:** check current official opening, entry and bag rules.

## 03 — “Where can we see the real Shanghai?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** everyday texture and context, often with an unstated wish to avoid tourist traps.

**Ask:**

- Do you mean architecture, street life, food, public space, or a neighborhood walk?
- Are you comfortable with places that are lived-in and not designed for visitor entertainment?
- Do you want a slower walk or a city-scale view?

**Say:**

`“Real” does not mean private access or an undiscovered place. In Shanghai, it usually means giving one neighborhood or public route enough time to notice how people move, shop, rest, and use the city.`

**Do not say:** `I can show you where locals really go.`

**Guest action:** follow resident, privacy and photography boundaries.

## 04 — “Can you book a restaurant / museum / ticket for us?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** reassurance that the next step is manageable.

**Ask:**

- Is the venue already chosen?
- What date, party size, language and dietary requirements matter?
- Do you need help understanding the official process or a different city-plan option?

**Say:**

`We can help you understand which choice fits your route and what to check. Reservations and payment need to be made directly by you or through the hotel’s own approved process.`

**Do not say:** `Leave it with us; we will arrange it.` unless your hotel independently offers and is authorized for that service.

**Guest action:** direct booking / hotel’s official concierge path.

## 05 — “We want to learn Tai Chi.”

**What the guest is really choosing:** body practice, a calm public-space observation, or a class—these are different.

**Ask:**

- Do you want to observe public practice, try a recreational beginner session, or seek serious training?
- Any injury, mobility, medical, language or child-safety constraints?
- Are dates fixed?

**Say:**

`A park observation and a class are different products. We can discuss the public-space context, but no class should be recommended as suitable, authentic, safe, or available unless it has passed the current provider review.`

**Do not say:** `I know a master / this will improve your health.`

**Escalate:** Human review for any named provider or physical participation request.

## 06 — “We are traveling with a child / older parent.”

**What the guest is really choosing:** control over pace, toilets, rest and exits—not simply family attractions.

**Ask:**

- What walking, stairs, stroller, rest, food or bathroom needs change the day?
- What time of day is realistically usable?
- Is rain/heat likely to change the group’s tolerance?

**Say:**

`We would build a shorter route with one anchor, an exit point, and a same-area alternative. We do not assume a venue is fully accessible or suitable for a particular child or health need.`

**Do not say:** `This route is fully accessible / child-safe.`

**Escalate:** Human review for material mobility, health or safety constraints.

## 07 — “We want the best food.”

**What the guest is really choosing:** a food style, comfort level and practical experience—not one universal ranking.

**Ask:**

- Are you looking for a specific Shanghai food tradition, a meal format, or a neighborhood atmosphere?
- Any allergies, restrictions, queues, reservation or language concerns?
- Is the meal a centerpiece or a flexible part of the day?

**Say:**

`The best choice depends on the route, your appetite, restrictions, queue tolerance and how much the meal needs to carry the day. We can frame that decision; allergy and restaurant information must be confirmed directly.`

**Do not say:** `This is the best / safe for your allergy / always easy to book.`

## 08 — “Can we do Shanghai, Suzhou and Hangzhou in three days?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** coverage versus depth, with transfer fatigue usually hidden.

**Ask:**

- Which city is the main reason for the trip?
- Are you willing to sacrifice Shanghai time for a Jiangnan contrast?
- What are your exact onward travel times?

**Say:**

`Three days can support a deliberate Shanghai-plus-one decision, but not a relaxed full experience of all three. Hangzhou and Suzhou answer different Jiangnan questions, so choose one or protect more time.`

**Do not say:** `They are all easy day trips.`

**Escalate:** Human review for a fixed multi-city sequence.

## 09 — “Where is the best photo spot?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** an image, often without recognizing weather, crowds, permission and physical effort.

**Ask:**

- Is this personal photography or commercial work?
- Does a tripod, drone, crew, lighting or a fixed sunset time matter?
- Can the guest accept a different visual story in rain, fog or crowds?

**Say:**

`We can help shape a public-space visual route, but no single photo outcome, access, equipment permission, weather or empty scene can be promised. Commercial filming and drones need their own current permissions.`

**Escalate:** Human review for commercial production, drone or equipment request.

## 10 — “Can you give us something more meaningful than ChatGPT?”

**What the guest is really choosing:** confidence in a route that has considered their trip rather than produced a fluent generic answer.

**Say:**

`A Deeper China starts with what changes your route: the time you really have, what you want to avoid, the pace you can sustain, and which part of the city you want to understand. A human-checked roadbook then records what was reviewed for your dates and what remains your direct action.`

**Do not say:** `AI is wrong / we know everything / this is fully verified.`

**Guest action:** complete a travel brief before any trip-specific review.
