# A Deeper China | Shanghai

## Research roadbook draft · V1.1

**Dates:** 10–13 September 2026  
**Travelers:** 2 adults  
**Profile:** First visit to China · architecture-led · unhurried pace  
**Assumption:** Three usable day blocks; arrival/departure times and hotel area still need confirmation

> This is a tailored research draft, not a booking, live-availability check or final human-checked itinerary.

## The route thesis

Read Shanghai through a few deliberate contrasts—river finance, one cultural frame, and lived twentieth-century streets—without turning a first visit into a checklist.

## Why this route fits

- It gives first-time visitors an orientation frame before asking for detail.
- It turns architecture into questions about money, migration, public life and reuse—not just façades.
- It keeps one main idea per day and leaves an exit when heat, rain or tired feet change the plan.

## What we deliberately leave out

- Covering every famous building in one district.
- Treating residential streets or private compounds as attractions.
- Promising a specific photo position, sunset or skyline condition.
- Adding a second museum simply because it is nearby.

## Day 1 — River city: Bund to Lujiazui

**Reading question:** How did one river hold treaty-port Shanghai and its finance-led future in the same view?

Use the Bund as a first architectural frame, then compare it with the public realm across the river. The purpose is to read two riverbanks, not to collect skyline photographs.

**Points:** The Bund architectural waterfront · Lujiazui public-realm / skyline view

**Fallback:** If heat, rain or crowding makes the crossing uncomfortable, keep the river question and shorten the outdoor segment rather than adding another viewpoint.

## Day 2 — One museum, one question

**Reading question:** Which single museum answers the travelers’ actual question about China?

Choose one museum branch after checking its current programme, opening arrangement and reservation rule. Leave the rest of the day for a slower neighborhood walk, meal or rest.

**Options:** Shanghai Museum, People’s Square · Shanghai Museum East Branch

**Decision rule:** These are alternatives, not two automatic stops. The correct choice depends on the hotel area, programme and energy level.

## Day 3 — Walkable modernity and the working waterfront

**Reading questions:** What did the former concession make possible, and how is that inheritance lived in now? How did industrial edges become civic, cultural and leisure space?

Use Wukang Road and the Hengfu area as a question-led residential and street-history walk. If energy and access make sense, add Yangpu Riverside to read industrial heritage and regeneration. Otherwise, stay within one walkable area and slow the day down.

**Boundaries:** No trespass, intrusive photography or framing ordinary residents’ lives as a spectacle.

## Checks required before delivery

- Museum opening arrangements, reservations and current programmes.
- Weather, shade, rest points and crowd load for the walking windows.
- Transport time, roadworks and temporary access restrictions.
- Cross-river route and viewing access.
- Residential boundaries and current conditions around Wukang Road.
- Riverside continuity, lighting and venue opening at Yangpu.

## Open questions

1. What time do you arrive on 10 September and depart on 13 September?
2. Which hotel area are you using?
3. Is normal city walking, including stairs and uneven pavement, comfortable?
4. Would you prefer a broad China-history museum or a current art/design exhibition?
