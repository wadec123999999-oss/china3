# A Deeper China | Quanzhou–Dehua draft

**Shape:** 2 days, built around A maritime system, not a monument list · Port, gate, bridge and river-sea movement · Religious plurality with etiquette.

## Why this Quanzhou
This is not a monument list. It reads a maritime system through public heritage, sacred etiquette, infrastructure and one production question.

## Suggested building blocks
- **A maritime system, not a monument list** — How did production, transport, belief and trade make Quanzhou a world port?
- **Port, gate, bridge and river-sea movement** — Where does trade infrastructure become visible?
- **Religious plurality with etiquette** — How do I visit sacred sites without consuming belief as spectacle?

## On-the-ground notes
- **Maritime-system framing card:** Quanzhou's value is the relationship between production, transport, marketing, institutions and social/cultural life, not an isolated temple count. **Check:** overpacking, interpretation.
- **Deji Gate / urban-port decision:** A possible way to make the relationship between city gate, maritime trade and urban circulation visible. **Check:** access, weather, walking.
- **Luoyang Bridge decision:** A transport-infrastructure component that can reveal the wider water-land network beyond the old-city core. **Check:** distance, weather, transport.
- **Kaiyuan Temple decision:** A major sacred/heritage component that can introduce Buddhist, urban and maritime-era layers when approached with current etiquette and access rules. **Check:** crowds, worship_etiquette, photography, opening_time.
- **Qingjing Mosque decision:** A key route into Quanzhou's transregional religious and trading connections, requiring a calm, context-led and etiquette-aware visit. **Check:** access_rules, worship_etiquette, photography.
