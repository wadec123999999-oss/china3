# A Deeper China | Beijing, first visit (human-check draft)

**For:** A first-time visitor with three full days, interested in history and architecture, comfortable with a full outdoor day.

## The idea

Do not try to consume Beijing as a sequence of monuments. Read one imperial system carefully, spend one day on the Wall as a landscape decision, then use a neighbourhood and a ritual landscape to see how the city works at human scale.

## Day 1 — The capital as an axis

Begin with the Palace Museum only if the named reservation and entry requirements have been rechecked for your passport and date. Keep the visit bounded: your question is how a capital makes hierarchy visible through gates, courtyards and direction.

After a rest, use Jingshan only if weather, crowding and mobility make the climb sensible. Its role is orientation, not another checkbox.

**Do not lock this day until:** current Palace Museum reservation, entry point, closure day, security requirements and weather are checked.

## Day 2 — The Wall as landscape, not a photo stop

Mutianyu is the working option only after a current transfer, weather, entry and return audit. The right choice depends on whether you value scenery, a recognisable first Wall experience, physical effort or reduced transfer complexity.

Build margin around the return. Do not add a major evening plan automatically.

**Decision questions:** comfort with stairs/uneven surfaces, cable-car preference, fear of heights, morning start tolerance, weather threshold and budget.

## Day 3 — Ritual and lived city

Use the Temple of Heaven to change the scale of the trip: ritual architecture inside a large public landscape. Then choose a hutong walk around the Drum Tower and Shichahai only if you want public streets, water and urban history—not access to private homes.

If palace architecture has already filled your attention, replace part of this day with 798·751 after checking the actual exhibition programme.
