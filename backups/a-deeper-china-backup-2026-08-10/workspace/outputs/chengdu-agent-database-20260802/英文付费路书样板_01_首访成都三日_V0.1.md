# A Deeper China | Chengdu, first visit (human-check draft)

**For:** A first-time visitor with three full days who wants pandas, food and a less rushed understanding of the city.

## The idea

Chengdu works when it has room to breathe. The Panda Base is one deliberate anchor, not an excuse to turn the rest of the trip into a frantic checklist. Tea, food and public space are part of the itinerary, not filler.

## Day 1 — Arrive into the city's pace

Keep the first full day intentionally light: a public-park and tea-time block, then one nearby neighbourhood or cultural lens. The aim is to understand how Chengdu uses time, not to prove that you have seen it.

**Human check:** arrival fatigue, weather, payment/data setup and the next day's named Panda Base reservation.

## Day 2 — Pandas, then less

Use the Panda Base only after its current official reservation, passport-matching and entry requirements are checked. Build the afternoon as recovery time plus one food or public-life layer. Do not promise animal activity, short queues or a particular gate.

For dinner, choose a meal format after confirming spice tolerance, dietary needs and whether a shared hotpot/chuanchuan meal is desirable.

## Day 3 — Choose one meaningfully different Chengdu

Choose **one**:

- Wuhou Shrine if Three Kingdoms memory is the actual draw;
- Du Fu Thatched Cottage for poetry, garden scale and a quieter cultural day;
- Wenshu area for a religious/neighbourhood texture;
- a current-city branch if design, shopping or a contemporary evening matters more.

If you want Dujiangyan, make it its own verified day rather than squeezing it into the city plan.
