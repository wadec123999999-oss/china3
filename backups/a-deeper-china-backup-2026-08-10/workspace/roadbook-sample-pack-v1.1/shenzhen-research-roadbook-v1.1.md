## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Use public evidence—hardware, interfaces, design and urban systems—to understand Shenzhen without promising private company access.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **What a Shenzhen tech trip actually is** — What are you trying to understand: hardware, consumer tech, manufacturing, AI, EVs, city systems or start-up culture?
  - **Anchor:** Define the technology question before choosing a place: hardware, interfaces, manufacturing, AI, EVs or city systems.
  - **Notice:** What is publicly observable, what is inferred, and what would require private permission.
  - **Stop rule:** A clear public evidence chain is enough; do not invent a company visit to make the route sound exciting.
  - **Main friction:** Corporate access, programme availability, translation and transport need human confirmation.
  - **Route shape:** Define one technology question—hardware, supply chain, interfaces, EVs or city systems—before choosing districts.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate transfers and cognitive load; a campus or company checklist is inefficient.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose one public technology layer and stop before adding another district.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed indoor markets, museums or design spaces.
- **Public-facing technology and city interfaces** — Where can I see technology as part of daily urban life rather than a corporate visit?
  - **Anchor:** Look for technology embedded in public life rather than expecting a corporate campus tour.
  - **Notice:** Interfaces, mobility, public services, design and who benefits from the system.
  - **Stop rule:** Keep the route public and observable; private access is a separate review path.
  - **Main friction:** Opening, language, data/privacy and access rules need checking.
  - **Route shape:** One public-facing technology sequence showing interfaces in everyday life, not private corporate access.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate transfer cost; public evidence matters more than the number of buildings.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one observable city-system layer.
  - **Rain version:** Use a confirmed indoor public venue or exhibition.
- **Nanshan and the innovation-city landscape** — How do I understand Shenzhen's growth without a campus tour?
  - **Anchor:** Use Nanshan to read growth through streets, housing, parks and work—not just a skyline.
  - **Notice:** How innovation is spatially organised and how ordinary users experience it.
  - **Stop rule:** One urban-system contrast is enough; do not turn the route into a campus checklist.
  - **Main friction:** Scale, heat, transfers and public access can make a short route fragile.
  - **Route shape:** One innovation-city landscape question, not a campus tour.
  - **Body and time budget:** Long transfers can overwhelm a short stay; public access must lead the design.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one public urban-system contrast and cut the far edge.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed indoor technology or design spaces.
- **Huaqiangbei: supply-chain observation, not a bargain hunt** — How do I explore an electronics market without being misled about prices, quality or sourcing access?
  - **Anchor:** Read Huaqiangbei as a supply-chain environment, not a bargain-hunting mission.
  - **Notice:** Component layers, signage, repair, sourcing language and the limits of casual purchasing.
  - **Stop rule:** Leave once the market logic is clear; do not imply quality, price or sourcing guarantees.
  - **Main friction:** Crowding, language, payment, authenticity and commercial pressure are real.
  - **Route shape:** One Huaqiangbei supply-chain observation, not a bargain hunt or sourcing promise.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate crowd and sensory load with language, pricing and quality friction.
  - **Low-energy version:** Observe one product/supply-chain question and leave before fatigue turns into impulse buying.
  - **Rain version:** Useful indoor branch, subject to current access and payment checks.

### Deliberate trade-off
- A technology route must distinguish what a visitor can observe publicly from what would require permission.
