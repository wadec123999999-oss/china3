## Day 1 — Hongya Cave is a visual entry point, not a shopping complex that must be explored from end to end.

### Reading the Night Skyline—Without Getting Lost Inside Hongya Cave

**Where:** Daijiaxiang — Hongya Cave exterior — Grand Theatre riverfront  
**Working duration:** about 150 minutes

From across the river, read bridges, water, cliffs, buildings and light as one urban cross-section.

**The one idea to carry:** Hongya Cave is a visual entry point, not a shopping complex that must be explored from end to end.

**Your observation task:** Point out the ridge, riverbank and bridges you travelled through earlier in the day.

**Reality check:** Night crowds can be heavy; lighting, drone performances and traffic controls depend on current official notices.

**Stop rule:** Stop once the visual geography is clear; do not spend the whole evening inside a shopping complex.

**Main friction:** Crowding, heat, stairs and confusing exits can erase the intended slow pace.

**Route shape:** A night orientation arc outside the commercial core, using one skyline contrast and an early exit.

**Body and time budget:** Longer evening window with crowd, stairs, heat and return-transport friction.

**Low-energy version:** Keep one clear external viewpoint and return before the route becomes a complex-navigation exercise.

**Rain version:** Use the most reliable public edge only if lighting, access and weather conditions support it.

**Confirm before release:** Preferred end time and crowd tolerance.

### The Multiple-Ground-Level Experiment

**Where:** Kuixinglou and connected high-level platforms  
**Working duration:** about 40 minutes

Test for yourself how a 22nd floor can also meet a street—the fastest way to understand Chongqing’s vertical logic.

**The one idea to carry:** Mountain terrain allows several street levels to be simultaneously true.

**Your observation task:** Guess the floor first, then leave through a different exit and test your assumption.

**Reality check:** Tell us about serious fear of heights in advance; the exercise does not require approaching an exposed edge.

**Stop rule:** End after one complete terrain experiment; do not stack several viewpoints just because they are nearby on a map.

**Main friction:** Step intensity, construction, rain and map distance need live checking.

**Route shape:** One complete high/low transition; stop after the terrain experiment is understood.

**Body and time budget:** Short on the map but stair and elevation intensity can be high; do not judge it by minutes alone.

**Low-energy version:** Use one platform and one lift/escalator option where confirmed; do not stack viewpoints.

**Rain version:** Remove the exposed transition unless same-day surface and construction conditions are acceptable.

**Confirm before release:** Tolerance for exposed heights.
