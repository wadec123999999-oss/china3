## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Use water, hills, tea and sacred landscape to create a genuine change of pace from Shanghai.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **Tea as landscape, labour and taste** — What does Longjing mean beyond buying tea or staging a ceremony?
  - **Anchor:** Use tea as landscape, labour and taste; do not reduce it to a purchase or staged ceremony.
  - **Notice:** Slope, cultivation, work, processing and how taste is explained.
  - **Stop rule:** Leave once the tea system is legible; private farmer access is not implied.
  - **Main friction:** Weather, season, transport, language and commercial claims need checks.
  - **Route shape:** One tea landscape/labour/taste sequence, not a buying stop or staged ceremony.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate transfer and hill exposure; local access and consent matter.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one public tea landscape and remove the second village or hill.
  - **Rain version:** Use a confirmed indoor tea or cultural layer; do not promise a private encounter.
- **Weather, mobility and seasonal friction** — Can this route survive heat, rain, crowds and hilly walking?
  - **Anchor:** Use Weather, mobility and seasonal friction to answer one concrete question, not to accumulate another stop.
  - **Notice:** Can this route survive heat, rain, crowds and hilly walking?
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.
  - **Main friction:** Current access, weather, crowding and walking conditions need confirmation.
  - **Route shape:** A weather, mobility and seasonal checkpoint before releasing a scenic route.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low sightseeing load; it protects the body and avoids false certainty.
  - **Low-energy version:** Shorten the route before removing the core question.
  - **Rain version:** Default branch when heat, rain, crowds or footing make the original plan fragile.
- **West Lake as a slow landscape** — How do I experience West Lake without treating it as a lap around a postcard?
  - **Anchor:** Choose one West Lake segment and stay long enough for water, hill and shade to change.
  - **Notice:** Edges, bridges, changing sightlines and how visitors share the landscape.
  - **Stop rule:** Do not complete a symbolic lap; stop when the lake’s rhythm is understood.
  - **Main friction:** Crowds, heat, weather, boat access and walking distance need checking.
  - **Route shape:** One West Lake landscape loop with a clear reading question, not a full postcard lap.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking with heat, shade gaps, crowds and water-edge fatigue.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose one shoreline section and protect a long rest.
  - **Rain version:** Shorten the lake edge and use a confirmed indoor cultural layer.
- **Wetland and quiet alternatives** — What should replace an over-crowded or weather-poor West Lake day?
  - **Anchor:** Use Wetland and quiet alternatives to answer one concrete question, not to accumulate another stop.
  - **Notice:** What should replace an over-crowded or weather-poor West Lake day?
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.
  - **Main friction:** Current access, weather, crowding and walking conditions need confirmation.
  - **Route shape:** A quiet-weather alternative built around wetland, public landscape or a lower-crowd loop.
  - **Body and time budget:** Lower crowd load but weather, footing and transport remain decisive.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep the shortest accessible loop and preserve recovery time.
  - **Rain version:** Use only after same-day surface and access checks.
