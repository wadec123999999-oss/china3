## On-the-ground notes
- **UNESCO cultural-landscape card:** The old tea forests are part of an evolved mountain system with forest, villages and long-standing Blang and Dai practices. **Check:** oversimplification, cultural_appropriation.
- **Understorey cultivation lens:** The heritage story is not simply old trees; it includes cultivation shaped by mountain ecology and forest conditions. **Check:** technical_translation, marketing_noise.
- **Tasting-learning card:** A visitor can learn to compare aroma, processing, batch information and seller questions without receiving purchase advice. **Check:** language, marketing, taste_variation.
- **Old-tree/provenance boundary:** Claims about tree age, origin, purity and producer identity cannot be authenticated through a travel database. **Check:** fraud, provenance, price.
- **Weather/mud/visibility card:** Rain, fog and mud can change route safety and visibility; scenic images are not operational evidence. **Check:** rain, fog, slips, landslide.
- **Low-impact trail conduct:** Staying on permitted routes, carrying waste and respecting fire/ecology rules is part of the product quality. **Check:** environment, route_access, fire_risk.
- **Public-space etiquette card:** Quiet conduct, consent and observing local rules protect residents, visitors and the landscape. **Check:** language, photography, cultural_norms.
- **Photo/ceremony refusal:** Ceremonies, homes, children and work processes cannot be assumed available for filming or visitor participation. **Check:** privacy, religious_respect, consent.
- **Three-night feasibility card:** Three protected nights allow one landscape thread plus a weather buffer; they do not justify a rushed multi-village sweep. **Check:** transfer_fatigue, weather, road_conditions.
- **Four-night slow observation card:** An additional night makes room for rest, a fallback day and a separately vetted low-impact interaction discussion. **Check:** provider_availability, seasonality.

## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Read Jingmai as a living tea-forest cultural landscape of forest, villages and mountain practice—not an old-tree-tea shopping trip.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **Tea landscape as a living system** — What connects forest, old tea groves, villages and livelihoods?
  - **Anchor:** Read tea as a forest, livelihood and settlement system rather than an old-tree-tea purchase.
  - **Notice:** Canopy, cultivation, labour, villages and what is publicly observable.
  - **Stop rule:** Leave once the tea-forest relationship is clear; do not imply authenticity or private access.
  - **Main friction:** Roads, weather, season, language and community rules need checks.
  - **Route shape:** One living tea-landscape question linking forest, old groves, villages and livelihoods.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate outdoor and transfer load with weather and ecology sensitivity.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one low-impact public landscape route and protect recovery.
  - **Rain version:** Remove exposed forest movement until footing and weather are checked.
- **Tea understanding and purchase boundary** — Is the visitor learning, tasting, buying or collecting?
  - **Anchor:** Use Tea understanding and purchase boundary to answer one concrete question, not to accumulate another stop.
  - **Notice:** Is the visitor learning, tasting, buying or collecting?
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.
  - **Main friction:** Current access, weather, crowding and walking conditions need confirmation.
  - **Route shape:** Separate learning, tasting, buying and collecting before any tea purchase is discussed.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low walking load but high trust, quality and payment friction.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep public tea ecology and tasting context; defer purchase decisions.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed indoor tea context.
- **Village etiquette and consent** — How can a visitor observe without treating residents as attractions?
  - **Anchor:** Observe village life through consent and public space, never as an arranged “authentic” encounter.
  - **Notice:** What can be seen respectfully and what belongs to residents alone.
  - **Stop rule:** Remove private homes, ceremonies and farmer access unless explicitly released.
  - **Main friction:** Consent, photography, translation and local rules require human checks.
  - **Route shape:** One village/public-space observation with consent, never an arranged “authentic” encounter.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low walking load but high privacy, translation and local-rules sensitivity.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep public observation and remove private homes or ceremonies.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed public/covered spaces only.
- **Arrival and protected pace** — Do the traveller's actual nights and transfer days make a mountain stay sensible?
  - **Anchor:** Protect nights and transfer time before promising a deep mountain stay.
  - **Notice:** Whether the actual itinerary leaves recovery time for a remote landscape.
  - **Stop rule:** If the timing is too short, recommend a shallower public landscape product instead.
  - **Main friction:** Road conditions, accommodation, connectivity and arrival/departure are decisive.
  - **Route shape:** A nights-and-arrival decision before promising a deep mountain stay.
  - **Body and time budget:** Transfer days can consume the experience; short stays may not earn the route.
  - **Low-energy version:** Add nights or keep Jingmai as a future product rather than compressing it.
  - **Rain version:** Use the base with the strongest transport and shelter fallback.
- **Walking, weather and ecology** — What risks and low-impact behaviour shape a safe day?
  - **Anchor:** Use walking and ecology to explain the tea forest while keeping the physical route low-impact.
  - **Notice:** Weather, ground, water, wildlife and how visitors affect the landscape.
  - **Stop rule:** Turn back early when conditions or body capacity change; the landscape is not a test.
  - **Main friction:** Rain, heat, footing, guides and emergency access need current confirmation.
  - **Route shape:** One low-impact walking/ecology route with a pre-set turnaround and emergency fallback.
  - **Body and time budget:** Weather, footing, water and access can change the route quickly.
  - **Low-energy version:** Turn back early; the landscape is not a fitness test.
  - **Rain version:** Remove the forest route until same-day conditions support it.
