## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Choose one water decision, one land-based landscape layer and enough recovery time; this is not a scenery conveyor belt.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **Li River as a water decision** — Do I want a cruise, a shorter river section, or land-based views?
  - **Anchor:** Make the water choice first: cruise, shorter river section or land-based views.
  - **Notice:** Scale, weather, boat rhythm and how the landscape changes from water level.
  - **Stop rule:** Once the chosen water experience answers the landscape question, stop adding another river product.
  - **Main friction:** Water conditions, weather, boarding, crowds and transfers require dated checks.
  - **Route shape:** A water decision between cruise, shorter river section and land-based views—not all three.
  - **Body and time budget:** Lower walking load but high weather, boarding and water-condition dependence.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose the simplest public water/land option with the clearest return.
  - **Rain version:** Do not release water movement without same-day weather and operating checks.
- **Karst landscape, not a checklist** — What relationship to the landscape do you actually want?
  - **Anchor:** Choose one land-based relationship to karst—walk, village edge, viewpoint or quiet observation.
  - **Notice:** Distance, cultivation, rock forms and human use rather than only panoramic beauty.
  - **Stop rule:** One landscape layer is enough for a short stay; do not chase every famous angle.
  - **Main friction:** Heat, rain, uneven ground and transport can change the usable route.
  - **Route shape:** Choose one relationship to the karst landscape—water, walking, farming or quiet observation—before choosing sites.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate outdoor exposure with heat, footing and weather sensitivity.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one land-based landscape frame and protect recovery time.
  - **Rain version:** Remove exposed movement and keep a confirmed indoor or low-risk landscape interpretation.
- **Yulong River and slow countryside movement** — How can I experience Yangshuo without assuming a bike or raft?
  - **Anchor:** Use Yulong River for slower countryside movement without assuming cycling or rafting.
  - **Notice:** Pace, water edge, farms, bridges and the difference between public scenery and private life.
  - **Stop rule:** Keep the activity within the group’s actual physical comfort; do not force a signature activity.
  - **Main friction:** Weather, water, equipment, consent and road safety need human checks.
  - **Route shape:** One slow countryside movement pattern without assuming cycling, rafting or private access.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low-to-moderate movement, with heat, road surface and transport friction.
  - **Low-energy version:** Use a short public countryside section and remove the second activity.
  - **Rain version:** Keep the land-based public route only if footing and access are acceptable.
- **Weather, water and route handoffs** — Can this plan survive rain, fog, water changes and intercity transfers?
  - **Anchor:** Treat weather, water and transfer conditions as route decisions, not footnotes.
  - **Notice:** What can be safely kept, shortened or removed on the actual date.
  - **Stop rule:** If the landscape cannot be enjoyed safely, switch to the land-based fallback rather than forcing the plan.
  - **Main friction:** Live conditions can invalidate a scenic sequence quickly.
  - **Route shape:** A release checkpoint for rain, fog, water changes and intercity handoffs.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low sightseeing load; it prevents fragile nature plans from becoming promises.
  - **Low-energy version:** Shorten the route before removing the core landscape question.
  - **Rain version:** Default branch when visibility, water or transfers become uncertain.

### Deliberate trade-off
- River, terraces and cycling are different physical products. A short stay should not promise all of them.

### Fallback logic
- If rain or conditions change, keep a land-based landscape route and remove the water segment rather than forcing it.
