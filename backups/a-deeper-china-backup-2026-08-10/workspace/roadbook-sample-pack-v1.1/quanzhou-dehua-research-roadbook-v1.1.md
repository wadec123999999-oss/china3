## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Read Quanzhou–Dehua as a maritime system connecting public heritage, sacred etiquette, infrastructure and production.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **A maritime system, not a monument list** — How did production, transport, belief and trade make Quanzhou a world port?
  - **Anchor:** Read Quanzhou as a maritime system connecting production, transport, belief and trade.
  - **Notice:** Ports, streets, inscriptions, food, worship and the movement of goods and people.
  - **Stop rule:** Leave after the system is legible; do not compress it into a monument checklist.
  - **Main friction:** Heat, transport, language and current opening conditions need checks.
  - **Route shape:** One maritime-system explanation linking production, transport, belief and trade.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking and attention load; separate city and Dehua transfers matter.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one public port/heritage layer and remove the farthest extension.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed indoor heritage or museum layers.
- **Port, gate, bridge and river-sea movement** — Where does trade infrastructure become visible?
  - **Anchor:** Use port, gate, bridge and river-sea movement to make infrastructure visible.
  - **Notice:** How goods and people moved, and which traces remain publicly readable.
  - **Stop rule:** Keep infrastructure connected to the city story; do not add disconnected photo stops.
  - **Main friction:** Heat, distances, tides/river conditions and access may alter the route.
  - **Route shape:** One port, gate, bridge or river-sea movement question—not a heritage checklist.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking with heat, traffic and waterfront continuity friction.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep the most legible public infrastructure section.
  - **Rain version:** Shorten the waterfront and preserve the infrastructure question indoors.
- **Religious plurality with etiquette** — How do I visit sacred sites without consuming belief as spectacle?
  - **Anchor:** Visit sacred sites as living places with etiquette, not as visual backdrops.
  - **Notice:** What visitors may observe, where they should pause and what should remain private.
  - **Stop rule:** One or two public sacred layers are enough; no private ritual or access promise.
  - **Main friction:** Dress, photography, worship schedules and local guidance require confirmation.
  - **Route shape:** One sacred-site sequence with etiquette and plurality explained before photography.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low-to-moderate walking; respect, dress and access matter more than distance.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose one public sacred layer and stop before adding another temple.
  - **Rain version:** Keep only confirmed public/covered access and remove exposed movement.
- **Arrival, weather and foreign-visitor friction** — Can the route work for actual dates, payment, transport and sacred/craft access?
  - **Anchor:** Treat arrival, payment, transport and sacred/craft access as part of the product, not admin afterthoughts.
  - **Notice:** Where a foreign visitor will face friction and what needs human help.
  - **Stop rule:** If access is not released, keep the public route and remove the implied encounter.
  - **Main friction:** Language, payment, transport, opening and consent are all live checks.
  - **Route shape:** A release checkpoint for dates, payment, transport, sacred access and craft boundaries.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low sightseeing load; it prevents conditional access becoming a promise.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep the public route and remove every unreleased encounter.
  - **Rain version:** Default branch when weather or access conditions are uncertain.
