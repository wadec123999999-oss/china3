## Day structure

### Day 1 — Imperial axis, read as a system
- **Imperial axis, read as a system**
  - **Reading question:** How do I make the Forbidden City meaningful rather than overwhelming?
  - **Route shape:** One imperial-axis reading with a clear entry, one contrast and a planned exit—not every hall.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking and high attention load; exposed heat, screening and queues can dominate the day.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one palace/civic sequence and use a short elevation reset; do not add another monumental site.
  - **Rain version:** Protect the confirmed indoor core and cut exposed park movement if weather or queues rise.
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.

### Day 2 — The Great Wall decision
- **The Great Wall decision**
  - **Reading question:** Which Wall section fits my day and ability?
  - **Route shape:** A Wall decision based on transfer, terrain, weather and turnaround—not reputation alone.
  - **Body and time budget:** High transfer and variable step load; the return plan matters as much as the ascent.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose the lower-load section or remove the Wall rather than turning the day into an endurance test.
  - **Rain version:** Do not release the Wall route without a current weather, surface and transport check.
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.

### Day 3 — Hutong as neighbourhood, not set
- **Hutong as neighbourhood, not set**
  - **Reading question:** How do I explore hutongs respectfully?
  - **Route shape:** One hutong neighbourhood sequence, not a commercial street checklist or a private-home tour.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking with crossings, shade changes, crowd and resident-privacy friction.
  - **Low-energy version:** Use the shortest public lane sequence and finish at a calm public stop.
  - **Rain version:** Keep one covered/indoor neighbourhood layer and avoid forcing a long lane walk.
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.

### Day 4 — Ritual, park and the lived capital + Water, gardens and imperial leisure
- **Ritual, park and the lived capital**
  - **Reading question:** Where can I see Beijing beyond palace architecture?
  - **Route shape:** One ritual-and-public-life contrast linking a civic or sacred space to how Beijing is used now.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low-to-moderate walking, with large open areas and weather exposure.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose one park or ritual anchor and preserve time for sitting rather than adding another district.
  - **Rain version:** Keep the indoor/covered interpretation and remove the long park segment.
  - **Stop rule:** Stop when the route question is answered; preserve time for rest and unexpected city life.
- **Water, gardens and imperial leisure**
  - **Reading question:** Do I need the Summer Palace after the Forbidden City?
  - **Route shape:** One water-and-garden contrast to the compact imperial axis, only when it adds a new question.
  - **Body and time budget:** Large-site walking and weather exposure can create hidden fatigue.
  - **Low-energy version:** Use the smaller central option or shorten the garden route before adding another landmark.

## On-the-ground notes
- **Palace Museum / Forbidden City:** The highest-density encounter with imperial spatial order, but its value comes from a clear reading question rather than trying to consume every hall. **Check:** time_specific_reservation, passport_identity, crowds, closed_day.
- **Jingshan Park:** A short elevation reset after the Palace Museum; useful to read the city from above rather than remain inside a sequence of halls. **Check:** weather, crowds, ticket_policy.
- **Mutianyu Great Wall:** A Wall day combining a well-preserved Ming section, mountain setting and multiple ascent/return choices. **Check:** weather, distance, reservation, transfer, cable_car_decision.
- **Badaling versus Mutianyu decision:** The meaningful choice is not which is 'best' but whether the visitor prioritises rail-like access/landmark recognition, mountain setting, or a particular physical challenge. **Check:** transport_variability, seasonality, crowding.
- **Drum and Bell Towers:** A strong entry point to the northern end of the historic city and to Beijing as a city organised by time as well as axis. **Check:** stairs, time_specific_hours, ticketing.
- **Shichahai–Yandaixiejie walking fabric:** An accessible way to connect water, old-city lanes, former residences and the Drum Tower area without reducing hutongs to a single photo street. **Check:** crowds, night_noise, commercialization.
- **Temple of Heaven:** Lets a visitor connect imperial ritual architecture with a large public park and everyday urban rhythms. **Check:** multi_zone_ticketing, opening_time, weather.
- **Qianmen–Dashilar:** A useful south-of-axis transition from monumental Beijing to commerce, streets and adaptive reuse. **Check:** tourist_density, commercial_noise, closing_times.
- **Summer Palace:** A contrast to the compact ceremonial logic of the Palace Museum: landscape, water, long movement and imperial retreat. **Check:** large_site_fatigue, weather, ticketing.
- **Beihai Park:** A central water-and-garden option that can reduce a dense north-city day without requiring a full Summer Palace commitment. **Check:** opening_time, weather, walking.
