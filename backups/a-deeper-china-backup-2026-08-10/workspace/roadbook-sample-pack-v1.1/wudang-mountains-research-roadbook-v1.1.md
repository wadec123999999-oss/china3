## On-the-ground notes
- **UNESCO heritage-system card:** The World Heritage property connects mountain landscapes with a long Taoist building tradition and surviving monumental architecture. **Check:** interpretation, religious_simplification.
- **Ming state-building lens:** A stronger story than generic mysticism: imperial patronage, construction and religious legitimacy shaped the complex. **Check:** historical_depth, translation.
- **Primary public heritage route:** One coherent public route can connect building, slope and viewpoint without turning a mountain day into a race. **Check:** crowds, wayfinding, stairs.
- **Low-exposure fallback route:** A useful roadbook needs a weather/energy fallback rather than a failed version of the main plan. **Check:** rain, fatigue, closures.
- **Two-night feasibility decision:** Two nights can support a focused heritage/mountain visit only if arrivals, weather and mobility remain workable. **Check:** transfer_fatigue, weather, stairs.
- **Three-to-four-night slow route decision:** Extra time creates a weather buffer and allows a separately vetted, low-intensity activity discussion. **Check:** seasonality, provider_availability.
- **Stairs and exertion disclosure:** Mountain routes can be stair-heavy and condition-sensitive; photos do not convey exertion. **Check:** mobility, cardiac_risk, fatigue.
- **Rain, storm and cold fallback:** Weather may alter visibility, comfort and operations; it must change the day plan, not merely add a warning. **Check:** rain, lightning, wind, cold.
- **Human-check release gate:** The safety and commercial quality of a roadbook depend on dated evidence, not confident prose. **Check:** dynamic_information, liability, scope.

## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Read Wudang through mountain architecture, Taoist rhythm, movement and etiquette—not a guaranteed martial-arts encounter.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **Public architectural route** — Which public heritage layer is meaningful without overpacking the day?
  - **Anchor:** Build a public architectural route around one heritage layer and a realistic body budget.
  - **Notice:** Mountain placement, thresholds, movement and what is genuinely public.
  - **Stop rule:** Do not add martial-arts or private instruction claims to fill the day.
  - **Main friction:** Steps, cable-car, weather, crowding and opening conditions need checks.
  - **Route shape:** One public architectural route, not every palace, temple or ridge in one day.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking and heritage attention load.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one public heritage anchor.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed covered/public heritage areas.
- **Mountain movement and weather** — What walking, stairs, cable-car and weather contingencies fit this visitor?
  - **Anchor:** Choose walking, stairs, cable-car and weather contingencies before choosing a scenic sequence.
  - **Notice:** How terrain changes the meaning and feasibility of the route.
  - **Stop rule:** Turn back before fatigue turns heritage into a safety problem.
  - **Main friction:** Mountain weather, step intensity, transport and recovery are decisive.
  - **Route shape:** One mountain movement plan with stairs, cable-car and weather contingencies.
  - **Body and time budget:** Potentially high steps and exposure; safety sets the route.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose the lowest-load public option and set a turnaround.
  - **Rain version:** Remove exposed movement until same-day footing and weather checks pass.
- **Heritage system, not fantasy** — What connects mountain, architecture, Taoism and Ming-era state building?
  - **Anchor:** Connect mountain, architecture, Taoism and state building without inventing a mystical encounter.
  - **Notice:** Spatial order, ritual boundaries and the difference between public history and belief practice.
  - **Stop rule:** Keep private ritual, healing and master access outside the route unless separately released.
  - **Main friction:** Etiquette, access, weather and interpretation need human review.
  - **Route shape:** One heritage-system explanation linking mountain, architecture, Taoism and state building.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate-to-high attention and transfer load; the mountain is not a backdrop only.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one public architectural layer and remove extra ascent.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed public/covered heritage access.
- **Arrival and protected pace** — How many nights and how much transfer/step capacity does this trip actually have?
  - **Anchor:** Use nights and transfer days to protect a mountain pace before adding another site.
  - **Notice:** What the body can actually do after arrival and before departure.
  - **Stop rule:** If the protected pace is not possible, do not sell a deep mountain product.
  - **Main friction:** Arrival time, accommodation, transport and step load are operational gates.
  - **Route shape:** A nights-and-transfer decision protecting early starts, recovery and realistic step capacity.
  - **Body and time budget:** Transfer and accommodation location determine feasibility.
  - **Low-energy version:** Add nights or reduce modules before adding mountain movement.
  - **Rain version:** Use the base with the strongest indoor and transport fallback.
- **Roadbook release and escalation** — What needs a human check, and when should the agent stop?
  - **Anchor:** Make the agent stop and escalate when the route depends on human access or safety judgement.
  - **Notice:** Which claims are research-only and which have dated evidence.
  - **Stop rule:** No release until the required human checks are complete.
  - **Main friction:** The entire product is sensitive to live conditions and access.
  - **Route shape:** A release checkpoint that tells the agent when to stop, escalate or keep the route public.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low sightseeing load; safety and evidence are the product boundary.
  - **Low-energy version:** Remove every unreleased practice, ritual or private-access claim.
  - **Rain version:** Default escalation branch when mountain conditions are uncertain.
