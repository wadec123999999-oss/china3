## Decision layer

**Route thesis:** Read Jingdezhen as a living system of material, process, kiln, labour and contemporary making—not a ceramic-shopping stop.

### Why this route is not a list
- The selected modules are a small information budget, not a ranking of every attraction.
- Each day should answer one question and leave a realistic exit for weather, energy or access changes.
- The route gets shorter when the guest has understood the idea; more stops are not automatically better.
- **Porcelain as a system** — What do material, labour, kiln, design and trade reveal together?
  - **Anchor:** Follow porcelain from material and labour through kiln, design and trade.
  - **Notice:** Clay, tools, heat, standards, labour and where value is added.
  - **Stop rule:** Leave when the production system is understood; do not replace it with shopping.
  - **Main friction:** Opening, transport, translation and commercial claims need checking.
  - **Route shape:** One porcelain-system question linking material, labour, kiln, design and trade.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate walking and high attention load; process matters more than venue count.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep one public material/process anchor and stop before adding another district.
  - **Rain version:** Strong indoor branch after current opening and programme checks.
- **Imperial kiln and technical history** — How did standards, management and technique shape porcelain?
  - **Anchor:** Use imperial kiln history to ask how standards, management and technique shaped objects.
  - **Notice:** Scale, process, fragments and the difference between historical evidence and brand storytelling.
  - **Stop rule:** One technical history layer is enough before contemporary making.
  - **Main friction:** Programme, opening, interpretation and walking conditions are dynamic.
  - **Route shape:** One imperial-kiln/technical-history sequence explaining standards, management and technique.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low-to-moderate walking with museum attention fatigue.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose one technical question and leave time to process it.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed indoor heritage layers.
- **Contemporary art and design ecology** — Do I want public art/design observation or a private artist encounter?
  - **Anchor:** Choose public art/design observation or a private artist encounter; they are different products.
  - **Notice:** How contemporary makers respond to material, place, labour and markets.
  - **Stop rule:** Do not imply a studio visit, workshop or purchase guarantee without written release.
  - **Main friction:** Access, language, consent, price and shipping/provenance need human review.
  - **Route shape:** One public art/design ecology question, never a private artist-access promise.
  - **Body and time budget:** Moderate programme and transfer uncertainty.
  - **Low-energy version:** Choose one confirmed public exhibition or district.
  - **Rain version:** Indoor branch after programme and access checks.
- **Arrival, language and delivery friction** — Can the client navigate, pay, communicate and receive work safely?
  - **Anchor:** Design the route around whether the client can navigate, pay, communicate and receive work safely.
  - **Notice:** Where a seemingly simple craft route creates real foreign-visitor friction.
  - **Stop rule:** Keep uncertain suppliers out of the guest-facing plan until checked.
  - **Main friction:** Transport, payment, translation, packaging and delivery conditions are live.
  - **Route shape:** A release checkpoint for arrival, language, payment and safe delivery of work.
  - **Body and time budget:** Low sightseeing load; it protects independent visitors from operational surprises.
  - **Low-energy version:** Keep the public route and remove every unsupported purchase or delivery promise.
  - **Rain version:** Use confirmed indoor public layers.
